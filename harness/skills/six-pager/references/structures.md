# Structures

Amazon publishes no template; Bezos's own words are "narratively structured six-page memos". The structures below are the ones former Amazonians and most guides converge on. Pick the one matching the memo's purpose, keep the section order unless the argument needs another, and rename headings to fit the content. A heading is a label; the topic sentence under it carries the claim.

Every structure opens with a short untitled or "Introduction" paragraph that states purpose, the ask and the headline, and closes the body before the appendix. Appendix and FAQ are outside the six-page limit and hold the data, computations, alternatives considered and answers to the hard questions. The body must stand on its own without the appendix.

## A Business review / operating plan (OP1, OP2, quarterly or annual review)

For a team reporting on a period and asking for approval of its plan, headcount or budget.

| Section | What it does | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Who the team is, what it owns, what this memo asks the readers to approve, and the headline result of the period in one or two numbers | 1 paragraph |
| Goals | Last period's goals, each with target, actual and status (a table is ideal), then next period's goals: specific, measurable, dated, owned. Bullets allowed | 0.5-1 page |
| Tenets | 3-6 principles the team uses to break ties when goals conflict, each specific enough that a reasonable team could choose the opposite ("We prefer a slower fix that removes a ticket type over a faster macro that answers it"). Generic virtues ("customer first") are not tenets. Bullets allowed | 0.25 page |
| State of the business | The period in numbers, with comparison to plan and prior period, the causes behind each move, and what the reader should conclude. Lead with the most important metric, not the best-looking one | 1.5-2 pages |
| Lessons learned | What went wrong and what went right, the root cause of each, and what the team changed because of it. Owned plainly, without blame or excuses | 0.5-1 page |
| Strategic priorities | The 3-5 things the team will do next, why these over the alternatives, the resources each needs, and what they will deliver against the goals. Say what the team will not do | 1.5-2 pages |
| Appendix / FAQ | Metric definitions, full tables, headcount detail, hard questions with answers | unlimited |

## B Decision or proposal

For asking the readers to choose: build vs buy, enter a market, change a process, fund a project. Most fit in three or four pages.

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| Introduction | The decision requested, the recommendation, the most important reason, and when the decision is needed |
| Background / why now | What changed that makes this decision necessary now; the cost of waiting |
| What we know | Facts with source and date; measured separated from estimated |
| Tenets | The criteria the decision should be judged on, agreed before the options are compared |
| Options | Three or more real options including doing nothing, each with cost, benefit and what it gives up. No straw men: each option should be one a reasonable person might pick |
| Recommendation | Why the recommended option wins on the tenets, and what must be true for it to work |
| Risks and what would change our mind | Failure modes, the early signal of each, and the evidence that would reverse the decision |
| Plan and resources | Owner, cost, first milestones with dates, who must be told |
| Appendix / FAQ | Computations, option detail, the strongest objections with answers |

## C New initiative

For proposing something new where the customer case must be made first. Same as B, with "Customer problem" (who the customer is, the problem, evidence it is real and how big) before the options, and "How we will measure success" after the plan. If the user wants the customer-facing framing to lead, that is a PR/FAQ, a different document: say so and offer it rather than bending this structure.

## Choosing when unclear

A period behind them and a plan ahead: A. A choice among alternatives: B. Something that does not exist yet: C. If two fit, pick the one whose ask matches what the readers must actually do at the end of the meeting.
